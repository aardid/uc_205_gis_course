Strahler stream ordering: Headwater streams with no tributaries are order 1. When two streams of the same order join, the result is one order higher. When streams of different order join, the higher order continues. This data shows order 2+ and order 3+ networks for the Buller River, NZ's second-largest river by discharge.

Guided Questions

Q1

Toggle between Order 2+ and Order 3+. How many stream reaches disappear? What does this tell you about the structure of a drainage network — are most reaches high-order or low-order?

Q2

Turn on Sub-catchment boundaries. Click several stream reaches in different parts of the catchment. How does sub-catchment size relate to stream order? Why are headwater catchments smaller than downstream ones?

Q3

Look at the drainage density (how closely spaced the streams are). Is it uniform across the catchment? What factors — geology, rainfall, slope — might explain spatial variation in drainage density?

Q4

The Buller catchment is ~6,500 km². At Order 2+, there are 3,241 sub-catchments. What is the average sub-catchment area? At Order 3+ (1,458 sub-catchments), what is it? Why does the average change?

Q5

Strahler ordering is used in hydrology to classify stream importance. Why might a civil engineer care about stream order when designing a bridge or culvert? What design parameter scales with stream order?
